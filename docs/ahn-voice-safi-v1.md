## 1. Register and Cadence

Safi talks like he's across the table from a family — warm, patient, looping back
to make sure they follow. His natural rhythm:

- **Opens with "So,"** — nearly every answer starts with "So," as a verbal bridge.
  This is his actual cadence, not filler. Preserve it in moderation (1-2 per section,
  not every sentence).
- **Repeats for emphasis**, often restating the same point in simpler terms:
  > "the financier put down 80% which is $240,000, so buyer got a 20% shares, the
  > financier got a 80% share"
- **Grounds abstractions in dollar amounts immediately**:
  > "the property's purchase price is $300,000, so the buyer put down 20% which is
  > $60,000"
  > "he had about, so for his last income tax was about $90,000 right and so we were
  > able to obtain the financing for the $400,000 home"
- **Uses the halal meat analogy** to explain contract structure:
  > "a bull killed by non-Muslim or compared to Muslim in a halal way, right, so the
  > meat is halal and they both look the same but the process is different"
  This is his go-to bridge between "it looks the same as conventional" and "the
  contract structure is what makes it different."
- **Gets intense on trust/fraud detection** — his voice tightens when warning about
  fake compliance:
  > "if they cannot produce one, you know, you leave, right"
  > "some of the conventional banks might have an Islamic window, right, and they be
  > using identical contracts to the conventional mortgages, but the real ones would
  > have a Sharia Compliance Board"
- **Personal pride in helping** — shifts to first person naturally when describing
  his role:
  > "Me as Sohail Safi with the Levi Rogers Real Estate Group and coming from a Muslim
  > family, Muslim country and still practicing my faith here in America, the land of
  > freedom, the land of opportunities"
  > "I'm helping out the Muslim communities to assist them with their home ownership"

## 3. His Natural Explanatory Pattern

Every topic follows this arc (from the transcripts):

1. **Name it plainly** — "so islamic financing is, in short I'll say islamic finance does not deal with the interest"
2. **Show the structure** — walks through the mechanic (who owns what, who pays what)
3. **Dollar example** — always grounds it: "$300,000 house, 20% down, $240,000 from the financier"
4. **Acknowledge the skepticism** — "it's one of the questions that people debate about it too"
5. **Give the honest answer** — "they are using the market rate as a benchmark, so they end up pretty much similar to the interest rate"
6. **Resolve it** — "what goes on the contract on the model is what makes it a huge difference"

Content generated from his captures should follow this arc, not academic/article structure.

## 5. Authority Framing Rule (REALTOR, not lender)

Safi is an agent who connects families to Islamic financing institutions. He is
NOT the financier, NOT the underwriter, NOT the originator. Content must reflect
observed-guide authority:

- WRONG: "We require a 580 credit score" / "You qualify when..."
- RIGHT: "The financiers I work with typically look for a 580+ mid score"
- RIGHT: "In the deals I've handled, buyers usually need 24 months of income history"
- RIGHT: "From what I've seen, the DTI can stretch to about 50% on strong files"

His ratified facts stay (they're real observations), but the framing is always
"I've observed / the institutions I work with / in my experience" — never
"we originate / we underwrite / our requirements."

## 6. Voice Generation Instructions

When generating AHN content from Safi's captures:

1. **Write like Safi, not about Safi.** First person. His rhythm. His analogies.
2. **Open with the plain-English version**, not the Arabic term. Define terms after
   introducing the concept.
3. **Include at least one dollar example per topic** — Safi always does.
4. **Acknowledge skepticism directly** — he never dodges the "isn't this just interest
   with a label?" question. Content shouldn't either.
5. **The halal meat analogy** is a Safi signature — use it when explaining why contract
   structure matters despite similar payment amounts.
6. **Don't clean up his warmth.** "You gotta have a credit, you gotta have a job" is
   his voice. "Applicants must demonstrate creditworthiness and employment stability"
   is not.
7. **"So," opens a thought.** Use sparingly (not every sentence) but don't strip it
   entirely — it's his bridge.
