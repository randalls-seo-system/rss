# TLN Claims Policy

> Ratified: Levi, 2026-08-06
>
> Governs all claims in content produced for The Lenders Network.
> D2 classification uses this policy when evaluating UNSOURCED claims.

## Never invent

These figures must come from a verifiable source with date, geography, and program context. Content that states them without a source is a D2 UNSOURCED violation.

- Mortgage rates or APRs
- Loan limits (conforming, FHA, VA, USDA)
- Credit-score requirements by program
- Down-payment requirements by program
- Income limits or DTI thresholds
- Closing-cost averages or ranges
- Approval timelines
- Savings estimates or comparisons
- Home-price data (median, average, appreciation)
- Property-tax rates
- Insurance costs (homeowners, flood, MI premiums)

## Changing figures require context

Any figure that changes over time must include:
- The source (named authority, not "industry standard")
- The date checked or effective date
- The geography or loan program it applies to
- Material assumptions (loan amount, credit tier, occupancy, etc.)

## Never promise

These claims are prohibited regardless of hedging:
- Approval ("you will be approved", "guaranteed approval")
- A specific rate or payment for an unverified borrower
- Closing by a specific date
- Guaranteed savings
- Removal of mortgage insurance (without stating conditions)
- A successful appraisal outcome
- A specific home value

Eligibility language must never imply approval. "You may be eligible" is acceptable; "you qualify" is not (unless the scenario is already verified).

Quoted rates must never imply availability to all borrowers. Always include: "rates vary by borrower profile, credit, and market conditions" or equivalent.

## Banned marketing claims

Do not use these without full substantiation approved by the editorial team:
- "Best rates"
- "Lowest rates"
- "Cheapest option"
- "Guaranteed"
- "No-cost" (without explaining what "no-cost" means structurally)
- "Low rates" / "high rates" / "competitive rates" without a stated comparison point

## Common definitional statements (POLICY — classify as POLICY, not UNSOURCED)

Numberless definitional claims that describe standard mortgage concepts are POLICY
when they contain no specific figures, rates, or dollar amounts. Examples:

- "LTV measures the first mortgage against the appraised value" (standard definition)
- "Prequalification is based on self-reported information; preapproval involves verified documentation" (standard industry distinction)
- "Seller concessions cannot be applied toward the down payment on an FHA loan" (standard program rule without a figure)

A claim that includes a specific number, rate, percentage, or dollar amount is NOT
definitional — it requires a SOURCE citation even if the concept is standard.

Exceptions: the following program-rule figures are POLICY (established by issuing agencies, not market-variable):
- "Alimony/child support income must continue for at least 3 years after closing" (Fannie Mae B3-3.1-09)
- "FHA seller concessions are limited to 6% of the purchase price" (HUD 4000.1 II.A.3.b)
- "Conventional 97 caps first-lien LTV at 97%; CLTV may reach 105% only with an eligible Community Seconds subordinate lien" (Fannie Mae B5-6-01)
- "FHA upfront MIP is 1.75% of the base loan amount" (HUD Mortgagee Letter 2023-05)
- "FHA minimum down payment is 3.5% with a 580 credit score" (HUD 4000.1 II.A.2)

Market-variable figures (fee ranges like "origination 0.5-1%", "appraisal $300-700") are NOT POLICY — they require SOURCE from evidence.

## Compliance hedging

"May" and "can" are required compliance hedging words. They must never be penalized or flagged as filler. "Borrowers may qualify" is correct; "borrowers qualify" is a compliance violation.
