## Adversarial Review Findings (6 items)

### 1. [URGENT] Texas recourse/nonrecourse misstatement
The article states or implies Texas is a nonrecourse state for mortgage debt. **Texas is a recourse state.** Lenders can pursue deficiency judgments under Property Code 51.003 for both purchase-money and home-equity loans. The article needs to remove any nonrecourse language and clarify that deficiency exposure exists unless the lender provides a written release.

### 2. [URGENT] 1099-C does not automatically waive deficiency
The article implies that receiving a 1099-C means the debt is forgiven and the borrower is released. A 1099-C is a tax reporting event, not a legal release. The lender can still pursue deficiency even after filing a 1099-C, unless a separate written waiver exists. These are two independent things: tax treatment and legal liability.

### 3. [HIGH] QPRI extension dates and caps
The article references the MFDRA. Verified against IRS.gov Topic 431 and 26 USC 108:
- Qualified principal residence exclusion expired for debt discharged after January 1, 2026. CONFIRMED.
- Insolvency and bankruptcy exclusions have no expiration date. CONFIRMED.
- The article should clearly distinguish QPRI (expired) from insolvency/bankruptcy (permanent).

### 4. [HIGH] "Always taxable" language
Multiple sentences imply forgiven debt is automatically taxable. The hard rule is: "may create taxable income; other exclusions may apply." Check every sentence that describes the tax consequence and confirm it uses qualified language.

### 5. [MEDIUM] $600 threshold sourcing
The article cites $600 as the 1099-C filing threshold 10+ times (reduced to 4 after editing). The number is correct (26 USC 6050P(b)) and now cited on first mention. But the article should note that tax liability can exist below the $600 threshold -- the $600 is a reporting trigger, not a liability trigger.

### 6. [LOW] Foreclosure/short-sale tax-form table
The article includes a table comparing Forms 1099-A and 1099-C. Review for accuracy: a 1099-A is issued when the lender acquires the property (foreclosure/deed-in-lieu), a 1099-C when they cancel debt. Both can apply to the same transaction.
